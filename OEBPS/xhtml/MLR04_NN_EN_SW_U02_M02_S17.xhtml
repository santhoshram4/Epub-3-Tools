How Sentences Work

Using Quotations

A direct quotation (or quote) uses exact words taken directly from a text. Including direct quotes in your writing shows that you know your topic well. A direct quote also helps your reader better understand the topic.

  •  Start your direct quote with an introductory phrase to let readers know where the text came from.

    • – According to the text, “__________.”
    • – The text says, “__________.”
    • – For example, “__________.”
    • – For instance, “__________.”

  •  When including direct quotes, use proper punctuation.

    • – Put quotation marks around the words from the text.
    • – Use a comma to separate the introductory phrase from the quotation.
    • – Capitalize the first word of the quotation if it is a complete sentence.
    • – Put end punctuation for the direct quote inside the ending quotation mark.

Look at the two examples of direct quotes. Notice the introductory phrases and proper punctuation.

Direct quote that is a complete sentence

For example, in “The Roots of Rap,” the author says, “Rap can be traced back to the griots, or storytellers, of West Africa in the 13th century.”

Direct quote that is not a complete sentence

In the text “Cy Thao, Story Painter,” the author says that Cy’s drawing led to “friendships made without words.”

Analyze an Explanatory Paragraph

Explanatory writing explains a topic or an idea. Strong explanatory writing presents facts and details in an organized and concise manner.

The TEES paragraph structure will help you write and organize your ideas clearly.

Topic sentence clearly states the main idea of the paragraph; it connects to the prompt.

Evidence includes facts, details, and quotations from the text related to the topic sentence.

Explanation connects the evidence to the topic sentence.

Sum-up sentence restates the topic sentence with some new information.

One reason people tell stories is they want to be remembered. For example, in the poem “The Storyteller Gets Her Name,” the speaker of the poem says, “I tell his tales and mine so someday when we join the elders, / my stories may be told, and his birds can take to the sky.” This quote explains that the speaker tells stories so their stories will continue to be told by others. This shows that the speaker wants to be remembered.
