Read Longer Words

If you come across a long, unfamiliar word that is difficult to read, look for “chunks” that you recognize. The chunks may be prefixes, suffixes, roots, or syllables. Then put the chunks together until you can read the word.

The steps in the Read Words Routine can help you read words with three or more syllables.

READ WORDS ROUTINE

  1. 1. Find word parts. Do you recognize any prefixes, suffixes, or roots?
  2. 2. Break the word into syllables. Does each syllable have at least one vowel sound?
  3. 3. Blend the syllables to read the whole word. When you put the syllables together, do you recognize the word?
  4. 4. Check that the word makes sense in the sentence.

Background Knowledge

Read to learn about the settings and details of The Last Cuentista.

NEW MEXICO

Part of The Last Cuentista takes place in New Mexico, a southwestern state in the United States that was once part of Mexico.

YUCATÁN PENINSULA

The cuentos (stories) in the novel are based on the oral traditions of the Yucatecos, or people from the Yucatán peninsula in Mexico. Yucatecos are Mayan, part of a culture that has existed for thousands of years. The Maya lived in the Yucatán peninsula long before Europeans from Spain colonized Mexico.

The Last Cuentista

by Donna Barba Higuera

Purpose for Reading

Reread The Last Cuentista to focus on characters, settings, and important events from this part of the story.

The year is 2061. Scientists predict that, for the first time, Halley’s Comet will collide with Earth. Twelve-year-old Petra Peña and her family have been chosen to board a spaceship to the planet Sagan before the comet hits. Petra must say goodbye to Lita, her beloved grandmother, in New Mexico.

  1. 1:Lita tosses another piñon log onto the fire. Sweet smoke drifts past us into the starry sky. Her knees crack as she sits back down on the blanket next to me. The cup of hot chocolate with cinnamon she’s made me sits untouched this time.

  2. 2:“I have something I want you to take with you on your trip, Petra.” Lita reaches into her sweater pocket. “Since I won’t be there for your thirteenth birthday . . .” She holds out a silver pendant in the shape of a sun. Its center is filled with a flat black stone. “If you hold it up to the sun, its light glows through the obsidian.”

    obsidian: dark, glassy volcanic rock formed from lava

  3. 3:I take it from her hand and hold it up, but there’s no sun. Only the moon. Sometimes I try to imagine I can see things I really can’t. But I’m sure a faint glow filters through the middle of the stone. I move the pendant back and forth. It disappears completely when I move it too far from the center of my vision.
  4. 4:When I look back, Lita is motioning to an identical pendant around her neck. “You know,” she says, “Yucatecos believe obsidian holds magic. A doorway to bring lost ones together.” She purses her lips. Her brown skin wrinkles toward her nose like cracked bark on a tree.
  5. 5:“They shouldn’t force me to go,” I say.
  6. 6:“You have to, Petra.” Lita looks away for a long time before speaking again. “Children are not meant to be separated from their parents.”
  7. 7:“You’re Dad’s parent. He should stay with you then. We all should.” Even as I say it, I know I sound like a little kid.

  8. 8:She laughs a deep, soft chuckle. “I’m too old to travel so far. But for you . . .Dios mío, a new planet! How exciting.”

    Dios mío: oh my god

  9. 9:My chin trembles and I bury my head into her side, squeezing her around the waist.

  10. 10:“I don’t want to leave you.”

  11. 11:Her stomach lowers with a deep sigh. Somewhere off in the desert behind Lita’s house, a coyote howls, calling for its friends. As if on cue, the chickens cluck, and one of her fainting goats bleats.

His father would bring crops, but he also brought great drought and death. One very hot day as Sun loomed over the nagual,” Lita waves her arms toward the heavens, “he challenged his father. Even though his mother begged him to stay with her forever, the young Fire Snake sped off toward his father.”

    drought: a long period without rain

  7. 18:Lita remains silent for a moment. I know the stall is part of her strategy to keep me in suspense. It works.
  8. 19:“Then what?”

  9. 20:She smiles and continues. “With his tail flaming behind him, Fire Snake gained speed until he could not slow himself. But as he approached his father, Sun, he realized his mistake. His father’s flames were far more powerful and stronger than anything else in this universe. The nagual looped around his father, speeding back toward his home, but it was all too late. His father’s fire had burned his eyes, so he could no longer see.” Lita clicks her tongue. “Pobrecito, blinded and moving so fast he could never slow down. Never able to find his mother.” She sighs. Now comes the part in all her stories where her voice becomes lighter, like she’s casually giving directions to the corner panadería. “So, every seventy-five years, he retraces the journey, hoping to reunite with her.” She points again at the fire snake. “Close enough to sense his mother, but never to embrace.”

    panadería: bakery

  10. 21:“Except this time,” I say, heat running up my back.

  11. 22:“Yes,” she answers, pulling me closer. “In a few days, the fire snake will finally find his mother. Y colorín Colorado, este cuento se ha acabado,” she says, ending her cuento.

    este cuento se ha acabado: ”this story is over” in Spanish

Explain Narrative Elements

Narrative elements are the parts of a story.

  • • A character is a person, animal, or creature in a story.
  • • The setting is when and where a story takes place.
  • • A conflict is a problem or challenge that characters face.
  • • The plot is the series of events that happen in a story. All narrative elements contribute to the plot.

How Sentences Work

Relative Pronouns and Relative Adverbs

Relative pronouns are words such as who, whose, whom, which, and that. They are often used to introduce a phrase that tells more about a noun in a sentence.

  • • Use who and whom when talking about people.
  • • Use that and which when talking about things or places.

Relative Pronoun

Example

who/whom

She was the only person who heard Lita’s story.

which

Lita told a cuento, which had been passed down by her grandmother.

that

This is the pendant that Lita gave Petra.

Underline the relative pronoun in the example.

Petra must say goodbye to her beloved grandmother, Lita, who lives in New Mexico.

Relative adverbs such as where, when, and why are used to introduce more information about a place, time, or reason.

Relative Adverb

Example

where

The story starts in New Mexico, where Lita lives.

when

When Petra goes to a new planet, Lita will stay on Earth.

why

I wonder why Petra’s family was chosen to go on the ship.
