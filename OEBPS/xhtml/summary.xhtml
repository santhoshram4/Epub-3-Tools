SUMMARY

  1. 1.1 Introduction to Tissues
    • • Why do some plants produce poisons, while others are edible and useful? Why are some plants carnivorous? How and why do plants respond to their environment? What is the future of tropical rain forests? What can be done about global warming and other environmental problems? This book addresses these questions and more about plants.
  2. 1.2 The Relationship of Humans to Their Environment
    • • Human populations have increased dramatically in the past few centuries, and the disruption of the balance of nature by the activities directly or indirectly associated with the feeding, clothing, and housing of billions of people threatens the survival of not only humans but other organisms as well.
    • •We are totally dependent on green organisms because they alone can convert the sun’s energy into forms that are usable by, and vital to the very existence of, animal life.
    • •We largely take plants and plant products for granted. Animals, animal products, many luxuries and condiments, and other useful substances such as fibers, lumber, coal, medicines, and drugs either depend on plants or are produced by them.
    • •To ensure human survival, everyone soon may need to acquire some knowledge of plants and how to use them. Plants will undoubtedly play a vital role in space exploration as portable oxygen generators.
    • •Teams of scientists are interviewing medical practitioners and herbal healers in the tropics to locate little-known plants used by local inhabitants before the plants become extinct.
  3. 1.3 Botany as a Science
    • •Botany, the study of plants, apparently began with Stone Age peoples’ practical uses of plants. Eventually, botany became a science as intellectual curiosity about plants arose.
    • Science involves the observation, recording, organization, and classification of facts. The verifying or discarding of facts is done chiefly from known samples. The scientific method involves following a routine series of steps and generally assuming and testing hypotheses.
  4. 1.4 Diversification of Plant Study
    • •The microscope has had a profound effect on studies in the biological sciences and led to the discovery of cells.
    • •Plant anatomy and plant physiology developed during the 17th century. J. B. van Helmont was the first to demonstrate that plants have nutritional needs different from those of animals. During the 17th century, Europeans engaged in botanical exploration on other continents and took plants back to Europe.
    • •During the 18th century, Linnaeus produced the elements of a system of naming and classifying plants.In recent years, molecular and cladistical investigations have resulted in modifications of Linnaeus’s system.
    • •During the 19th century, plant ecology, plant geography, and plant morphology developed, and by the beginning of the 20th century, genetics and cell biology became established. Much remains yet to be discovered and investigated.
