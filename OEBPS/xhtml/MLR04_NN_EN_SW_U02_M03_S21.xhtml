Genre

Read to learn about the genre of The Last Cuentista.

Science fiction (“sci-fi”) is a type of imaginative fiction inspired by science and technology.

  • • Settings include planets and outer space, the future, and worlds with advanced technology.
  • • Characters might be people, aliens, robots, or creatures.
  • • Events often involve space travel and saving humans from harm.
  • • Conflicts might come from natural disasters, threats from aliens, mysterious planets, a powerful and harmful government, or computers taking over.

The Last Cuentista

by Donna Barba Higuera

(PART 2)

Purpose for Reading

Read to learn what Petra thinks and feels as she’s about to leave Earth.

  1. 40:The departure facility isn’t more than an old, converted ranger station beyond some gates to the National Park.
  2. 41:I’m supposed to feel happy my parents were chosen to go to the new planet, Sagan. But I feel like I’ve been given the last glass of water on Earth and I’m just gulping it down while everyone watches.

  3. 42:I look up at the comet and wince. I hate you.

    wince: shrink back from something painful or scary

  4. 43:Like ants on an orderly march to our hole, my family and I walk quietly across the grass field with several scientists and one other family with a blond teenager. As we get closer, instead of the cement commercial launch pad I expect, there’s just freshly cut grass.
  5. 44:Mom speaks quietly. “You won’t even know any time has passed when we’re up there. There’s nothing to be nervous about.” But when I look over, I catch her scrunching her eyes tight and shaking her head like that will somehow make this all go away. “And when we arrive to Sagan,” she continues, “we’ll start over, like on a farm. There will be others around your age.”
  6. 45:She can’t make this better. I don’t want any new friends ever again. I even had to set Rápido loose behind Lita’s house. Maybe my tortoise will somehow survive the comet’s hit deep within his burrow, and live out his life without me.
  7. 46:“This is stupid,” I mumble. “Maybe I should just tell them about my eyes so they won’t let us on the ship.”
  1. 47:Mom and Dad exchange a glance. Mom takes me by the elbow and pulls me aside. She smiles at the other family as they pass.
  2. 48:“What are you doing, Petra?”
  3. 49:I feel tears rising up. “What about Lita? It’s like you don’t even care.”
  4. 50:Mom closes her eyes. “I can’t tell you how hard this is for all of us.” She lets out a breath and looks at me. “I’m sorry for how this is hurting you, but this is not the time.”
  5. 51:“When will be the time?” I say too loudly. “Hundreds of years from now when she’s already gone?”
  6. 52:The blond boy now ahead of us glances back. His dad elbows him, and he turns back around.

  7. 53:“Petra, we can’t know exactly what will happen.” Mom glances furtively at the other family. She grabs her braid and twists its end in her hand.

    furtively: in secret

  8. 54:“I think you’re lying.”
  9. 55:Mom glances at Dad and lays her hand on my arm. “In this moment, Petra, the world does not revolve around you. Have you thought of how others might be feeling?”
  10. 56:I almost say the world might not revolve at all anymore, but my arm vibrates. I look over and see Mom is trembling.
  11. 57:She points back in the direction we came. “Did you notice the people waiting outside the gates?”
  12. 58:I look away. I don’t want to remember the woman pulling off her wedding ring and pushing her baby forward, toward the armed guard. “Please, please,” she mouthed over and over as we drove right through the gates. Just like the tracking had predicted, that young family and hundreds of others had somehow figured out the government was hiding something out here.
  13. 59:“They’d give anything to be onboard with us.” Mom leans down, her eyes boring into mine. “Do you want to leave?”
  14. 60:I think of the mom with her baby, and if I never saw Dad or Mom or Javier again.
  15. 61:“No,” I answer.
  16. 62:A woman and a young girl approach holding hands. The girl has a silver spiraled horn jutting out the top of her head from her hoodie. As they pass, she makes an obvious head turn and stares at me suspiciously.
  17. 63:“Suma, tttccch,” her mom whispers, and the girl looks away.
  18. 64:Mom glances in their direction, and I know she’s seen them watching us too. “So, can you please keep your opinions to yourself for now?”

How Words Work

Homophones

Homophones are words that sound the same but have different meanings and spellings. Read about three homophones.

Word

Meaning

Example

they’re

contraction for they are

They’re walking ahead of us, toward the ship.

their

pronoun that means “belonging to them”

Mom glances in their direction, and I know she’s seen them watching us too.

there

adverb that describes a place

You won’t even know any time has passed when we’re up there.

Complete each sentence with they’re, their, or there. Underline the context clues that help you choose the correct homophone.

  2. 2. __________ names are on the list for the first ship.
  3. 3. He said that __________ planning to leave soon.
