How Words Work

Similes and Metaphors

In Catching a Storyfish, the author creates vivid images by making comparisons. You’ve already learned about two types of comparisons: similes and metaphors.

A simile is a comparison of two different things using the words like or as.

He’s got whiskers as long as the Mississippi.

A metaphor is a comparison of two different things, but the words like or as are not used. In a metaphor, the poet or author says that one thing is another.

Mama says, “You’re my little parakeet.”

Read each example. Label it as a simile or metaphor. Then explain the meaning.
