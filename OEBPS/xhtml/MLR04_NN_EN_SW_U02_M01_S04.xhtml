Explain Figurative Language

Figurative language is made of words and phrases that mean something different from their literal, or exact meanings. Authors use figurative language to create images, emotion, and meaning for readers.

Some types of figurative language show creative comparisons of unlike things.

  • • A simile is a comparison of two things using the word like or as.
  • • A metaphor also compares two things but does not use like or as.

Read the two examples of figurative language from Ancestory. For each example, underline the two things being compared. Then put an S next to the example if it shows a simile or an M if it shows a metaphor.

Though like houses, these works of art took a long time to make. (paragraph 10)

However, these ancient artworks remain—distant memories in stone of what came before us. (paragraph 21)

How Words Work

Morphology: Prefixes im-, non-

Many words can be broken down into parts that give clues about the word’s meaning.

  • • A base word is a word that can stand on its own.
  • • A prefix is a word part that comes before a base word.
  • • A suffix is a word part that comes after a base word.
  • • Prefixes and suffixes change the meaning of the base word.

You can put together the meanings of a word’s parts to figure out the meaning of the complete word.

impossible = not + able to happen = not able to happen
