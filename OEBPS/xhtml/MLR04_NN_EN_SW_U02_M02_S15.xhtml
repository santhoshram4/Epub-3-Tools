Read Longer Words

If you come across a long, unfamiliar word that is difficult to read, look for “chunks” that you recognize. The chunks may be prefixes, suffixes, roots, or syllables. Then put the chunks together until you can read the word.

The steps in the Read Words Routine can help you read words with three or more syllables.

READ WORDS ROUTINE

  1. 1. Find word parts. Do you recognize any prefixes, suffixes, or roots?

  2. 2. Break the word into syllables. Does each syllable have at least one vowel sound?

  3. 3. Blend the syllables to read the whole word. When you put the syllables together, do you recognize the word?

  4. 4. Check that the word makes sense in the sentence.

Compare Point of View and Perspective

Point of view describes the type of narrator a story or poem has.

  • • A story or poem told from a first-person point of view has a narrator who is a character in the story. The narrator describes events and their own observations, thoughts, and feelings. First-person narrators use pronouns such as I, me, we, or us.
  • • A story or poem told from a third-person point of view has a narrator who is not a character in the story. This type of narrator describes events using pronouns such as he, she, they, and them. They can also describe the inner thoughts of more than one character.
  • • The narrator in a poem is called the speaker.
