Unpack the Prompt

An essay is a series of paragraphs that work together to explore a topic or idea.

A literary analysis is a type of explanatory writing that explores a literary text, such as a novel, a story, a drama, or a poem. For example, it might explain the text’s characters, events, or themes.

Literary Analysis Essay Rubric

Category

Expectation

Thinking and Ideas

The essay includes:

  •  A theme of the story.
  •  Text evidence that supports ideas.

Organization

The essay includes:

  •  A clear and interesting introduction.
  •  A focus statement that responds to the prompt.
  •  Two well-organized body paragraphs that each support the focus statement.
  •  A conclusion that restates the most important ideas and explains why they matter.

Language and Mechanics

The essay includes:

  •  Precise language that explains the topic.
  •  Correct spelling, capitalization, and punctuation.
  •  Different sentence types that make the writing smooth and interesting.

Examine a Model

Like an explanatory essay, a literary analysis essay can have three main parts.

  • • The introduction is the first paragraph. It introduces the topic and includes a focus statement that states the main idea of the essay.
  • • Body paragraphs are in the middle of the essay. They provide evidence and explain how the evidence supports the focus statement.
  • • The conclusion is at the end. It restates the most important ideas in the essay and leaves the reader with something to think about.

Read the model essay below. This essay was written in response to the prompt: Write a four-paragraph literary analysis in which you describe how the impact of storytelling in Catching a Storyfish reveals a theme of the story.

Why do stories matter? Can they impact how we feel? In Catching a Storyfish, storytelling is important to the characters, especially Katharen. Storytelling has a big impact on Katharen because it helps her feel close to others and better during hard times, showing the theme that stories can bring comfort and connection.

When Katharen tells stories, she feels close to her family. For example, in “Keet- Keet Parakeet” Katharen says that she likes to tell stories to “make Daddy lean in, and hold me octopus-tight, stories to make Mama’s eyes shine birthday-candle bright.” This shows that Katharen enjoys seeing the excitement that her parents feel listening to her stories. Telling stories connects Katharen to her family.

Similarly, storytelling helps Katharen and her grandfather through hard times. When her grandfather becomes sick, storytelling helps them cope. For instance, in “Keet’s Wish,” Katharen says, “I won’t give up. I’ll tell him stories. I’ll make the sad go away.” This example reveals how Katharen’s stories help them both feel better. Katharen’s stories make even hard times better.

In all, the impact of storytelling shows the theme that stories are a powerful way for people to feel connected and comforted. In Catching a Storyfish, Katharen uses stories to connect with her parents and stay strong when her grandfather is sick. Stories are not just fun. They help us show love, stay close, and support each other through challenging times.

Read the alternative introduction to the model essay.

It is a cool fall evening, and my family and I are sitting around a firepit, listening to the stories my grandfather and his brother share. The way their stories bring us together makes me think of Katharen, a character in Janice N. Harrington’s novel in verse Catching a Storyfish. Storytelling has a big impact on Katharen because it helps her feel close to others and better during hard times, showing the theme that stories can bring comfort and connection.
