Draft an Introduction

An introduction tells the reader the topic and focus of the essay. It should “hook” the reader and make them want to keep reading.

To write an introduction:

  • • Start with an interesting idea or question about the topic to grab the reader’s attention. This is called the hook.
  • • Write a more specific statement that connects your hook to the topic in your prompt.
  • • End with your focus statement. This statement should clearly answer the question in your prompt.

Draft a Body Paragraph

The body paragraph of a literary analysis essay contain evidence and explanations to support the focus statement. To write strong body paragraphs:

  • • Make sure the evidence and explanation in each body paragraph support the topic sentence. Follow the TEES paragraph structure.
  •  Topic sentence that tells what the paragraph will be about
  •  Evidence or example from the text that supports the topic sentence
  •  Explanation that connects the text evidence to the topic sentence
  •  Sum-up sentence that ends your paragraph with a closing idea

When using direct quotations:

Start with an introductory phrase.

  • • For example, _____.
  • • The author says, _____.

Use correct punctuation.

  • • Place the words from the text inside quotation marks (“ ”).
  • • Use a comma after the introductory phrase.
  • • Capitalize the first letter of the quote if it is a complete sentence.
  • • Place ending punctuation inside the final quotation mark.

Draft a Conclusion

A strong conclusion wraps up a literary analysis essay in three ways:

  • • It restates the focus statement in a new way.
  • • It briefly summarizes the key points in the body paragraphs.
  • • It helps readers understand why the ideas are important.
